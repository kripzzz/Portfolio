Designing an ERP System for a Hospital ERP & Industry 4.0: From Strategy to Execution Executive Summary This report presents the perspective of an ERP implementation consultant advising hospital leadership on the design of an enterprise resource planning system. The focus is on module selection, master data governance, transaction design, procurement controls, and system integration for a 200-bed multi-specialty hospital operating OPD, IPD, diagnostics, pharmacy, and OT services 24×7. The recommended ERP core comprises Patient Management & EMR, Billing/Finance & TPA, Pharmacy & Inventory, Diagnostics & Lab, and Staff Rosters & Payroll. These modules connect the hospital’s clinical, financial, inventory, diagnostic, and workforce processes. The Patient Master serves as the foundation, supported by eight master data sets that must be validated and populated before go-live. The design prioritises three high-volume processes: patient registration-to-discharge, pharmacy procure-to-dispense, and diagnostic order-to-result. A value-based approval workflow for drug and equipment procurement, supported by a hard budget check, is recommended to strengthen financial control and procurement governance. Two integrations are identified as the highest-risk areas: Pharmacy & Inventory with Finance/Billing, and Patient Management/EMR with the Insurer/TPA interface. These should receive priority during technical design to minimise revenue leakage, inventory mismatches, claim delays, and patient-service issues. Overall, the proposed ERP aims to create a connected hospital enterprise with stronger control, visibility, and accountability. Industry Overview The engagement covers healthcare   specifically a 200-bed multi-specialty hospital offering OPD, IPD, diagnostics, pharmacy and OT services. From an implementation standpoint, a hospital is one of the more demanding enterprises to design for: it operates 24×7, carries life-critical inventory (drugs, blood, implants), bills through multiple payer types (self-pay, corporate, government scheme, insurance/TPA), and must remain compliant with clinical, financial and regulatory requirements (NABH accreditation, GST, drug-control regulations, patient data privacy). A gap in master data or a weak workflow does not just erode margin here   it carries direct patient-safety exposure, which materially raises the bar for this design relative to a typical retail or manufacturing rollout. The as-is state at most hospitals is a set of functional silos   the front desk, pharmacy, laboratory and billing counter each running separate registers or point systems, with patient details, insurance approvals and drug consumption re-entered at every counter. This is the standard root cause of delayed billing, medicine stock-outs and duplicate or lost patient records. The to-be state recommended in this report replaces these silos with a single shared patient-centric database   one system, one database, one source of truth   so that a patient record, once created, flows automatically through consultation, diagnostics, pharmacy, billing and discharge. 2. Business Process Modelling End-to-End BPMN 2.0 Process Documentation Business Context Organization For this modelling exercise, the client is a Multi-Specialty Hospital, a mid-sized tertiary care hospital, has engaged ABC Consulting to document and automate its Patient Discharge process ahead of an ERP implementation. The process is cross-functional, which makes it a suitable candidate for ERP-enabled workflow integration. Process The Patient Discharge Process begins when a patient becomes clinically ready for discharge. It brings together clinical clearance, billing and insurance processing, pharmacy fulfilment, patient counselling and final bed-release coordination. The submitted model includes four swim lanes: Physician, Billing / Finance, Pharmacy and Nursing. Purpose The purpose is to ensure that a patient is discharged only after clinical, financial and medication-related requirements have been completed. From an ERP perspective, the process also provides a clear opportunity to connect information that is often handled by separate departments and systems. Business Problem The main business challenge is coordination. A discharge can be delayed when clinical clearance, insurance authorization, final billing, medication dispensing or transport arrangements are not completed in the right sequence. The process also contains an exception path for rejected cashless claims, making timely escalation important. A fragmented process can lead to longer patient waiting time, repeated follow-ups and poor visibility for management. Business Analyst perspective. The model is not simply a sequence of clinical tasks; it shows where rules, exceptions, ownership and information transfer affect process performance. This makes it suitable as a baseline for ERP/EHR workflow automation. Process Scope End-to-End Process Flow The model follows a logical sequence: clinical readiness assessment, discharge decision, discharge order and prescription, final billing, cashless/self-pay decision, insurance authorization or self-pay calculation, payment and clearance, medication verification and dispensing, patient counselling, transport and bed release, patient discharge. The 'No' branch at the clinical gateway loops back to treatment and re-evaluation, while the rejected-claim branch converts the case to self-pay and revises the bill. BPMN 2.0 Process Diagram The submitted Bizagi model contains the required core BPMN constructs, including a start/end flow, activities, sequence flows, decision points, swimlanes, a data object and an exception path. The assignment specifically requires at least 10 activities, 2 decision gateways, 3 swimlanes, 2 business rules and 1 exception path. Business Rules These are the rules that actually shape the decision points in the process I've written them the way I'd expect to see them in an ERP workflow configuration, since that's ultimately what they'll need to become: The patient cannot move past the discharge order step until the physician has explicitly confirmed clinical fitness for discharge this is the gate that starts everything else.
